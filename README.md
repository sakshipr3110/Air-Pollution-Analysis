# 🌍 Global Air Pollution & Health Risk Analysis

## 📌 Project Overview

Air pollution is one of the most critical environmental challenges affecting human health and ecosystems worldwide.  
This project performs a comprehensive analysis of global air quality data to identify pollution patterns, high-risk regions, and key contributing pollutants.

The analysis focuses on Air Quality Index (AQI) and major pollutants such as **PM2.5, Ozone, NO2, and CO**, uncovering meaningful insights to support environmental awareness and decision-making.

📊 This project demonstrates end-to-end data analysis, including data cleaning, exploratory analysis, visualization, and insight generation.

---

## 🎯 Objectives

- Identify the most polluted countries and cities  
- Analyze AQI distribution across regions  
- Understand the contribution of different pollutants  
- Examine relationships between pollutants  
- Highlight regions requiring urgent environmental action  

---

## 🧠 Key Insights

- The **Republic of Korea** records the highest average AQI (421), followed by **Bahrain, Mauritania, and Pakistan**, indicating severe air pollution in specific regions.

- Multiple cities in **India**, including **Dataganj, Rohtak, Faridabad, and Churu**, show AQI levels of **500**, representing extremely hazardous air quality conditions.

- While a large proportion of cities fall under **Good (9,688)** and **Moderate (9,087)** categories, a significant number still experience **Unhealthy to Hazardous conditions**, highlighting widespread environmental concerns.

- **PM2.5 is the dominant pollutant** (68.88), significantly higher than Ozone (35.23), NO2 (3.08), and CO (1.38), making it the primary contributor to poor air quality.

- A very strong correlation (**0.98**) between AQI and PM2.5 confirms its critical role in determining air quality levels.

---

## 🚨 Implications

- High AQI levels in multiple countries indicate serious **public health risks**, particularly in densely populated regions.

- Cities with AQI values of 500 represent **critical pollution hotspots** and require immediate intervention.

- Controlling **PM2.5 emissions** can significantly improve overall air quality.

- Air pollution is a **global issue with regional intensity**, requiring targeted and coordinated environmental policies.

---

## 📊 Analytical Highlights

- Strong correlation between AQI and PM2.5 indicates **particulate matter dominance**

- Moderate correlation between CO and NO2 suggests **common emission sources (vehicles and industry)**

- Negative relationship between Ozone and NO2 reflects **atmospheric chemical interactions**

- Presence of repeated AQI values (500) indicates **extreme pollution events or capped measurements**
